### 1. Expect 70-80% AI coverage once a page ranks in Google's top 10  `80.7`
*core · content insights · source 80*

Grow and Convert report a specific conversion rate between Google rank and AI presence: they see 70% to 80% coverage in ChatGPT and Perplexity for keywords where their clients rank in Google's top 10 or top 3 spots. That gives a usable planning number. Earning a top-10 position for a bottom-of-funnel query is not a separate project from AI visibility, it is most of the work, and the remaining 20% to 30% is where content specificity and differentiation decide the outcome. It also sets an expectation for reporting: a client ranking top three and still absent from AI answers is an outlier worth investigating rather than the normal state.

> "coverage in ChatGPT and Perplexity for keywords"

**Evidence:** Grow and Convert report 70%-80% coverage in ChatGPT and Perplexity for keywords where clients rank in Google's top 10 or top 3.

**Tools:** ChatGPT, Perplexity

**Pitfall:** Treating GEO as a separate workstream with its own tactics when most of the result comes from ranking the same bottom-funnel pages. The inverse error is assuming top-10 rank guarantees a mention; roughly a quarter of the time it does not.

**Apply at Pabau:** Pabau's AI-visibility reporting should sit next to Google rank for the same bottom-funnel keywords. Any keyword ranking top three with no AI mention gets a content-specificity review rather than a technical one.

**Apply anywhere:** Expect most keywords you rank top-10 for in Google to also produce AI mentions, and treat the exceptions as a content-specificity problem to investigate individually.

### 2. Expect 77% AI mention rate from page one, 82% from top three  `86.1`
*core · content insights · source 86*

Grow & Convert analyzed 400+ bottom-of-funnel keywords and prompts across 16 of their own clients and found the single most consistent driver of AI visibility is traditional Google ranking. When a client ranked on Google's first page for a keyword, they appeared in ChatGPT and Perplexity responses for the matching prompt 77% of the time. Ranking in the top three positions pushed that to 82%. They also found a separate correlation between Ahrefs Domain Rating and how often ChatGPT and Perplexity mentioned a client. Their explanation is mechanical, not mysterious: ChatGPT's web search calls third-party engines including Bing and Google, and Perplexity's CEO has said publicly they use third-party crawlers alongside their own. The engines look at what ranks and synthesize it. So ranking is the exposure event, and no LLM-specific tweak substitutes for it.

> "they show up in ChatGPT and Perplexity responses"

**Evidence:** 400+ bottom-of-funnel keywords across 16 Grow & Convert clients: 77% mention rate when ranking page one, 82% when ranking top three, plus a positive correlation between Domain Rating and mentions.

**How to do it**

1. Pull your bottom-of-funnel keyword list and record current Google position for each.
2. Run the matching prompt in ChatGPT and Perplexity and log whether your brand appears.
3. Split the results by position band: top 3, positions 4-10, page 2 and beyond.
4. Expect roughly 80% mention rate from the top 3 and roughly 77% from page one; treat a much lower rate as a content-specificity problem, not a ranking problem.
5. Spend the next quarter's budget on moving page-two BOFU keywords onto page one rather than on llms.txt, FAQ blocks or schema.
6. Re-measure monthly and chart mention rate against position to prove the correlation holds in your own category.

**Tools:** ChatGPT, Perplexity, Ahrefs, Traqer

**Pitfall:** The correlation was measured on bottom-of-funnel, product-related prompts only. Informational prompts often get answered without any web search, so ranking page one for them buys no mention at all.

**Apply at Pabau:** David should stop treating AI visibility as a separate program at Pabau. Track Google position for the bottom-of-funnel terms (clinic software comparisons, competitor alternatives, aesthetic practice management) and measure ChatGPT and Perplexity mentions against those positions; the ranking work is the AI work.

**Apply anywhere:** Stop treating AI visibility as a separate program. Track Google position for your bottom-of-funnel commercial terms and measure ChatGPT and Perplexity mentions against those positions; moving keywords onto page one is what buys the mention.

### 3. Expect 86% of citations for product prompts to come from industry sites  `84.2`
*core · content insights · source 84*

Grow & Convert classified 2,440 domains cited across 120 product-intent prompts for five clients: Toro TMS, Wrike, Axial, Climb Hire and ServiceTitan. About 59% of citations (1,444) were direct industry sources, meaning vendors that sell the product being discussed. About 26% (649) were industry-adjacent publications such as Transport Topics in trucking. Only about 14% (347) were general domains like Forbes, Wikipedia, Reddit, Quora or YouTube. Combined, 86% of citations sat inside the industry ecosystem. The pattern held across all five clients and across four B2B brands plus one B2C. The practical read is that vendor blogs are the dominant citation source for commercial prompts, which makes your own content a first-class GEO asset rather than a fallback.

> "about 86% of all citations came from within the industry ecosystem"

**Evidence:** 2,440 cited domains across 120 prompts and five clients: 59% direct vendors, 26% industry-adjacent, 14% general.

**Tools:** Traqer

**Pitfall:** The dataset is agency clients who already rank well on Google, so the vendor share partly reflects their existing rankings rather than a universal LLM preference.

**Apply at Pabau:** Pabau's GEO budget should sit mostly on pabau.com content and on aesthetic-industry publications, not on chasing Forbes or Reddit placements that account for a seventh of citations in comparable verticals.

**Apply anywhere:** For product-intent prompts in a defined vertical, expect roughly six in seven citations to come from vendors and trade publications. Spend your GEO budget on your own content and on industry titles rather than mainstream outlets.

### 4. Expect ChatGPT to need third-party mentions where AI Overviews accept owned content  `81.7`
*core · content insights · source 81*

Grow and Convert flag an asymmetry inside their own case study. Constitution Lending built visibility almost entirely from content on its own site, with no citation outreach started yet, and it appears strongly in Google AI Overviews and Perplexity. It appears less frequently in ChatGPT. Their reading is that ChatGPT prioritizes diverse brand mentions across third-party sources and places less emphasis on owned content alone, while AI Overviews is effectively summarizing organic results and Perplexity lists sources that correlate with page-one rankings. The practical consequence is sequencing. Owned bottom-of-funnel content buys AI Overviews and Perplexity visibility on its own, and ChatGPT needs a second workstream of mentions on the sites it already cites.

> "ChatGPT prioritizes diverse brand mentions and places less emphasis on owned content"

**Evidence:** Grow and Convert report Constitution Lending appears less frequently in ChatGPT than in Google AI Overviews and Perplexity, and attribute it to having done no citation outreach yet.

**How to do it**

1. Score visibility separately per engine rather than as one blended AI visibility number.
2. If AI Overviews and Perplexity are strong but ChatGPT is weak, read that as a third-party mention gap, not a content gap.
3. Run your target prompts through ChatGPT and list every domain it cites in the answers.
4. Deduplicate that list and rank the domains by how often they appear across the prompt set.
5. Pitch inclusion only on those domains, using the listicle or roundup format they already publish.
6. Ask for the brand name plus the differentiator sentence, and accept an unlinked mention if a link is refused.
7. Re-run the ChatGPT prompt set six to eight weeks after each placement lands.
8. Keep publishing owned bottom-of-funnel content in parallel, since that is what carries AI Overviews and Perplexity.

**Tools:** ChatGPT, Perplexity, Traqer

**Pitfall:** Reading weak ChatGPT numbers as a signal that the content strategy failed, and rewriting pages that are already winning on the other engines.

**Apply at Pabau:** Pabau likely faces the same split. Owned template and comparison pages should carry AI Overviews and Perplexity, while ChatGPT visibility will need placements on the aesthetics and practice-software roundup sites that ChatGPT actually cites.

**Apply anywhere:** Track AI visibility per engine. Strong AI Overviews and Perplexity numbers with weak ChatGPT numbers usually mean you lack third-party mentions, not that your content is wrong, so open a separate workstream aimed at the domains ChatGPT cites.

### 5. Expect ChatGPT to skew toward the most-mentioned products on page one  `89.2`
*core · content insights · source 89*

Grow and Convert compared the average Page One Popularity of the products ChatGPT recommended against the average for all products Google's page one listed. For five of the six categories tested, ChatGPT's picks had a higher average popularity than Google's own page-one average. The reason is not that Google ignores popular products, it lists them too. It is that Google's listicles pad themselves out with dozens of obscure tools, which drags the average down, while ChatGPT names only seven or eight and biases them toward genuinely well-known names. The only exception in their data was 'best time clock software'. The practical read is that mention frequency across the whole category, not the ranking position of any single page, is the variable that tracks with being recommended.

> "ChatGPT tends to recommend the most popular products from Google's first page"

**Evidence:** Five of six B2B SaaS categories showed ChatGPT's recommended products with a higher average Page One Popularity than the average product Google listed; 'best time clock software' was the exception.

**Tools:** ChatGPT

**Pitfall:** Treating this as causation. The authors are explicit that ChatGPT is not reading Google; the two systems both scrape the web and land on overlapping conclusions.

**Apply at Pabau:** Pabau's AI-visibility goal should be raw mention count across aesthetic-practice software roundups, not just ranking pabau.com. Getting named in ten more third-party listicles moves the needle more than one more owned page.

**Apply anywhere:** Your AI-visibility goal should be raw mention count across category roundups, not just ranking your own site. Being named in ten more third-party listicles moves more than one more owned page.

### 6. Expect Perplexity and Google AIO to lift before ChatGPT does  `82.1`
*core · content insights · source 82*

Grow and Convert report that across dozens of client brands, visibility on Perplexity and Google AI Overviews rises before ChatGPT visibility does. Their hypothesis is that Perplexity and AIO are more heavily influenced by traditional web search results, so they behave like search summarizers and reflect ranking gains almost as soon as the rankings land. ChatGPT blends more of its training data into responses, which biases it toward larger, better-known brands and slows the payoff for a smaller name. The practical effect is a sequencing expectation: after a page starts ranking, check Perplexity and AIO first for evidence the work landed, and treat flat ChatGPT numbers in the same period as normal rather than as failure. It also means a small brand should not judge a GEO program on ChatGPT alone.

> "visibility on Perplexity and Google AIO often increases before ChatGPT"

**Evidence:** Grow and Convert's observation across dozens of brands they track in Traqer; offered as a hypothesis, not measured data.

**Tools:** Perplexity, ChatGPT, Traqer

**Pitfall:** Reporting only ChatGPT share of voice makes an early-stage program look dead. The tell is Perplexity and AIO mentions climbing on the same prompt basket while ChatGPT stays at zero.

**Apply at Pabau:** Pabau should split AI-visibility reporting by engine and read Perplexity and Google AI Overviews as the leading indicator after a blog or template page starts ranking. Expect ChatGPT mentions to lag by months because it favors larger established brands.

**Apply anywhere:** Split AI-visibility reporting by engine. Read Perplexity and Google AI Overviews as the leading indicator once a page starts ranking, and expect ChatGPT to lag because it leans on training data that favors bigger brands.

### 7. Expect bottom-funnel clicks to survive AI answers while top-funnel collapses  `107.16`
*core · content insights · source 107*

Grow and Convert's mechanism for why funnel stage now decides content value is about what the searcher needs after the answer. For a query like ten HR trends this year, an AI Overview or ChatGPT gives a synthesized answer and there is nothing left to click for, so traffic from top-of-funnel rankings has plummeted. For a query like best HR software for small business or Gusto vs Rippling, the user is actively evaluating and wants to see feature details, screenshots and pricing, and eventually book a demo or trial. Their phrasing is that LLMs can help start bottom-of-funnel buying journeys but cannot finish them. They report users still click through, still convert, and the conversion rate on those clicks has not changed. So bottom-of-funnel content is structurally protected in a way top-of-funnel content is not.

> "LLMs can help start BOTF buying journeys but they can't finish it"

**Evidence:** Grow and Convert report the conversion rate on bottom-of-funnel clicks has not changed despite AI Overviews.

**How to do it**

1. Classify every planned topic by asking whether a complete AI answer would end the user's task.
2. Drop or deprioritize topics where the answer is the whole job, such as trend roundups and best-practice guides.
3. Keep and expand topics where the user must see your product to decide: comparisons, alternatives, pricing, category best-of.
4. On those pages, include the things a synthesized answer cannot supply, meaning screenshots, pricing detail and a demo path.
5. Track click-through and conversion rate separately for the two groups rather than assuming both are declining.
6. Move genuinely top-funnel material to channels where the impression itself has value, such as social or video.

**Pitfall:** Reading a sitewide traffic decline as an AI problem across the board leads to defensive rewrites of everything. The decline is concentrated in top-of-funnel pages, and conversion rate on bottom-funnel clicks is unchanged.

**Apply at Pabau:** Pabau's comparison, alternatives and pricing-adjacent pages are the protected asset. Those pages should carry real screenshots, specific feature detail and a clear demo path, since that is what a user clicks through for.

**Apply anywhere:** Ask of each topic whether an AI answer completes the user's task. If it does, deprioritize it. If the user must still see a product to decide, that page is protected and worth investing in.

### 8. Expect content detail, not backlinks, to decide an LLM recommendation  `80.4`
*core · content insights · source 80*

Grow and Convert say there is a strong correlation between ranking in Google and being mentioned by AI tools, but with an important difference: chatting with an AI leads these platforms to recommend brands based on the details of the content and far less on superficial SEO metrics like backlinks. The reasoning is that the model is matching a stated user situation against published specifics, and a link count says nothing about whether a company serves that situation. This partly contradicts the usual authority-first planning: they point out that a Google search for 'best SEO agencies' would just serve the usual high domain authority suspects, whereas ChatGPT named a smaller specialist because it knew the user ran a business funding company.

> "far less on superficial SEO metrics like backlinks"

**Evidence:** Grow and Convert contrast a hypothetical Google search for 'best SEO agencies', which returns high-DA incumbents, with a real ChatGPT answer that put their smaller agency first for a business funding company.

**Tools:** ChatGPT

**Pitfall:** Reading a weak backlink profile as the reason you are missing from AI answers, then spending the quarter on links while the content stays generic.

**Apply at Pabau:** Pabau does not need to out-link the large incumbent software brands to be recommended. The lever is publishing detail about aesthetic and healthcare practice workflows that those brands do not describe.

**Apply anywhere:** Do not assume a thin link profile is what keeps you out of AI answers. Models weigh the specificity of your published detail against the user's stated situation more than they weigh authority metrics.

### 9. Expect only 40% of ChatGPT citations to rank for its fan-out queries  `87.1`
*core · content insights · source 87*

Grow and Convert tested 100 buying-intent prompts in the ChatGPT browser UI and compared cited sources against the Google and Bing results for each fan-out query. Only 27% of cited sources ranked anywhere in Google's first 10 pages for those queries. Bing was 23%. After accounting for the roughly 10% overlap between the two engines, about 40% of cited sources appeared in either engine. So more than half of ChatGPT's citations for product prompts do not rank in the first ten pages for the queries ChatGPT itself ran. This contradicts the common claim, recorded elsewhere in this base, that ranking for fan-out branches reliably earns citation. Plan AI visibility on the assumption that fan-out ranking is a partial lever, not the mechanism.

> "only 27% of cited sources ranked on Google"

**Evidence:** 100 buying-intent prompts, 83 of which triggered a web search; 27% Google exact-URL overlap, 23% Bing, ~40% combined.

**Tools:** SerpAPI

**Pitfall:** Teams build a content plan around scraped fan-out queries and then cannot explain why citations do not follow. The signal is a page ranking top-3 for the fan-out query and still absent from the answer.

**Apply at Pabau:** David should stop treating fan-out query lists as a Pabau content plan. Keep the aesthetic-practice fan-outs as a coverage check on existing articles, not as the reason to commission new ones.

**Apply anywhere:** Stop treating scraped fan-out queries as a content plan. Use them as a coverage check on pages you already have, because only about 40% of what an LLM cites ranks for those queries at all.

### 10. Favor bottom-funnel keywords because the searcher still has to visit you  `121.7`
*core · content insights · source 121*

Grow and Convert argue the AI shift strengthens the case for buying-intent keywords rather than changing it. AI Overviews and tools like ChatGPT and Perplexity answer informational queries directly, so the traffic top-of-funnel content used to generate is shrinking. Bottom-of-funnel queries behave differently. When someone searches 'project management software for agencies' or 'HubSpot alternatives', an AI tool can summarize the options, but the searcher still has to visit sites to evaluate a product, request a demo or start a trial. They add a second effect: even when the AI answers directly, being named or recommended in that output still shapes the decision, and that is far more likely when you have strong content ranking for the keyword than when you have nothing. So the same pages do double duty as click targets and as citation sources.

> "Bottom of the funnel content is more resilient to these changes"

**Evidence:** Grow and Convert's observation that AI Overviews now appear for a large percentage of searches while bottom-funnel evaluation queries still require a site visit.

**Tools:** ChatGPT, Perplexity

**Pitfall:** Reading AI resilience as a reason to stop measuring clicks on those pages. Impressions and citations can rise while clicks fall, so a page can be working and still look flat in a clicks-only report.

**Apply at Pabau:** Pabau should shift blog investment toward comparison, alternatives and per-treatment software pages, which survive AI summarization, and stop commissioning general explainers that an AI Overview answers outright.

**Apply anywhere:** Weight your content toward evaluation queries rather than definitional ones. AI answers the informational query outright, but a buyer comparing products still has to open the vendor's site.

### 11. Fix a weak topic with content, product framing and citation outreach  `85.4`
*core · concrete actions · source 85*

Grow and Convert name the three actions that move a specific weak topic, using Mirascope's low visibility on context engineering platform as the worked case: publish more content on that topic, explain clearly how the product supports that use case, and conduct citation outreach on the articles that already rank and get cited for it. They are explicit that these actions will have no impact on visibility for the neighbouring topic, prompt engineering tools. That is the point of measuring per topic: it tells you where the three actions should be pointed. The product framing step is the one most teams skip. If the product pages never use the topic's own language, an LLM has nothing to attribute to you even when it reads your site.

> "conducting citation outreach on relevant articles"

**Evidence:** Grow and Convert prescribe exactly these three actions for Mirascope's context engineering platform gap and state they will not move prompt engineering tools visibility.

**How to do it**

1. Pick the single lowest-band topic with real commercial value and treat it as a standalone campaign.
2. Run the topic's prompt basket and record every domain cited in the answers, not just whether you appear.
3. Publish two to four articles that address the topic directly, using the topic's exact phrasing in the title and first sentence.
4. Rewrite the relevant product or feature page so it states plainly how the product serves that specific use case, in the topic's language.
5. Sort the cited domains into ones that already list you and ones that do not.
6. Email the authors of the cited articles that omit you with a concrete, checkable reason to add you, such as a feature the article's criteria section requires.
7. Re-run the same basket 30 days later and check band movement on that topic only, ignoring the brand aggregate.
8. Repeat on the next lowest-band topic rather than expecting spillover.

**Tools:** Traqer

**Pitfall:** Publishing content for the weak topic while the product pages still describe the product in the strong topic's vocabulary. The model reads your site, finds no support for the claim, and keeps citing competitors.

**Apply at Pabau:** For a weak Pabau topic, pair the new blog articles with an edit to the matching feature page so it names the use case in the searcher's words, then pitch the third-party comparison articles already cited for that topic.

**Apply anywhere:** For a weak topic, pair new articles with an edit to the matching product page so it names the use case in the buyer's own words, then pitch the third-party articles already cited for that topic.

### 12. For B2B SaaS, weight AI-visibility budget toward Claude, not just Google  `25.13`
*core · concrete actions · source 25*

Charles describes two concrete shifts in budget/strategy allocation for the AI-search era. First, link-building spend is dropping from near-100% of external-authority budget to roughly a 50/50 split with 'mention building' — he notes clients now sometimes complain when a placement gives them a hard link instead of the unlinked brand mention they actually wanted, since mentions feed entity/consensus-building for AI systems. Second, optimization work is becoming genuinely multi-channel: instead of reverse-engineering only Google, his team now reverse-engineers ChatGPT, 'Reddit AI,' Gemini, and Google in parallel. Critically, the channel split isn't fixed: for most clients Google search still gets about 90% of the work, but for a B2B SaaS client specifically, Claude is named directly as likely deserving a higher share of the strategy budget than traditional Google would otherwise take, implying B2B SaaS buyers/researchers disproportionately use Claude versus other assistants.

> "if we're doing B2B SaaS, for example, then actually Claude is probably"

**How to do it**

1. Segment your external-authority acquisition plan into two named buckets, 'link building' and 'mention building,' rather than treating all PR/outreach as link building.
2. For mention-building placements, brief writers/PR contacts to name the brand clearly in text without requiring a hyperlink, since the goal is entity association and consensus, not PageRank flow.
3. List every AI/answer-engine surface relevant to your buyers, at minimum Google/AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Reddit-sourced AI answers, instead of only tracking classic Google rankings.
4. Because Pabau is B2B SaaS, explicitly allocate a larger-than-default share of the AI-visibility budget/testing effort to Claude specifically, rather than defaulting to Google-only or an even split across all assistants.
5. Periodically query each assistant (Google AI Mode, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) with buyer-style prompts about practice management software to see which brands each one surfaces (inferred, ties to the AI Mode attribute-testing method used elsewhere in this transcript).
6. Track over time whether the balance of effort across channels should shift, since the 90/10 Google-to-other split is described as a current default, not a fixed rule, particularly for B2B SaaS.

**Tools:** ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Claude

**Pitfall:** Defaulting to one generic channel split (or a Google-only focus) for every client regardless of vertical — Charles explicitly flags B2B SaaS as an exception where Claude should get more weight than the standard allocation, so applying a fixed ratio across all niches misses where your specific buyers actually are.

### 13. Gate core IP behind a human exchange, not AI  `29.9`
*core · general insights · source 29*

Hank argues the 'old compact' of SEO - you provide content, the searcher provides a visit - is breaking down as AI surfaces summarize and answer without sending traffic, so every publisher now has to choose a mix of discovery (fully public, freely AI-citable content) versus discretion (content gated behind an interaction). His rule of thumb: publish freely up to the edge of your actual core IP - the specific, unique expertise or data that differentiates you - but require a human-led exchange to access that core layer, since if you just publish it and an AI ingests and restates it, the ingestion immediately devalues the expertise behind it.

> "we provide content in exchange for a visit"

**Evidence:** Hank's stated framework, applied across his current consulting clients: as AI Overviews and chatbots summarize content without sending a visit, the traditional 'content for a visit' compact breaks, so every publisher must decide how much information stays fully public versus reserved for a human-led exchange once a user reaches the edge of the site's real, differentiated IP.

**Apply at Pabau:** Segment Pabau's content into a public-education tier (freely AI-citable, drives discovery) and a gated-expertise tier (specific implementation detail, proprietary benchmarks, personalized guidance) that only unlocks through a real interaction like a demo or sales conversation, so AI summarization can't fully substitute for engaging with Pabau directly.

**Apply anywhere:** Segment your content into a public-education tier (freely AI-citable, drives discovery) and a gated-expertise tier (specific implementation detail, proprietary benchmarks, personalized guidance) that only unlocks through a real interaction like a demo or sales conversation, so AI summarization can't fully substitute for engaging with your site directly.

### 14. Get every review-directory listing ranking on Google, not just populated  `08.7`
*core · concrete actions · source 08*

LLM answer engines are described as searching the open web for 'consensus' across many third-party review sources rather than trusting a single platform, so a brand needs reviews spread across multiple relevant directories (for home services: Google, Thumbtack, Yelp, Facebook), not concentrated on one. Critically, having reviews on a directory isn't enough — that specific listing page also has to actually rank on Google (the source's benchmark is top 20-30) or it's invisible to an LLM's own research process; a listing buried at position 33 or 56 gets missed even with genuine reviews on it. The fix given is to build links directly at the specific buried listing URL, not just the homepage, to push it back into visible range.

> "those directory listings rank on Google"

**How to do it**

1. Identify every third-party review platform relevant to your industry (for B2B SaaS: G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, GetApp, Software Advice, etc.).
2. Spread active review-generation effort across at least 3-4 of these platforms rather than concentrating on just one, since LLMs cross-reference multiple sources for consensus.
3. For each directory listing, search '[brand name] reviews' on Google and note whether that specific listing ranks within roughly the top 20-30 results.
4. For any listing buried past that range, build links directly to that specific listing page (not your homepage) to push it into visible range.
5. Re-run the '[brand name] reviews' search periodically to audit which listings currently rank on page one versus which still need link support.
6. Treat this as an ongoing maintenance task rather than a one-time project, since directory rankings and LLM indexing both drift over time.

**Pitfall:** Assuming a large review count on a single platform is sufficient for AI visibility — brands with reviews concentrated on only one source were observed to 'sink to the bottom' on LLM answers, since models cross-reference multiple platforms for consensus rather than trusting one source alone.

### 16. How ChatGPT, Grok, and Claude actually source citations  `40.6`
*core · content insights · source 40*

Kasra's technical model of how LLMs source citations, based on his own testing: ChatGPT effectively runs three tiers - a cached-answer tier for extremely common queries such as 'who is the president,' a bulk Common Crawl scraping tier for general web content that is visible in your own server logs as aggressive Common Crawl bot activity, and a live 'page bot' tier that fetches a specific page or Wikipedia entry in real time for a less-common query. Grok and Perplexity, by contrast, lean much more heavily on live search with comparatively little caching. Kasra validated this by deliberately deleting a set of test pages: Grok and Perplexity dropped the pages from citation almost immediately, while ChatGPT and Claude kept citing the deleted content for three to four extra weeks, consistent with serving from a cache until the next Common Crawl refresh drops it. Separately, Claude's underlying citation source was reverse-engineered by a researcher going by 'Rolo'/Apex Marketing and found to be Brave Search rather than an independent index.

> "ChatGPT has three different bots, to my knowledge"

**Evidence:** Kasra's own controlled experiment: built and ranked test pages, tracked their AI-citation status, then deleted the pages and monitored each platform - Grok and Perplexity lost citation almost immediately, while ChatGPT and Claude continued citing the deleted pages for roughly three to four weeks, and Claude's underlying source was separately reverse-engineered as Brave Search.

**Apply:** Don't assume removing or updating old content changes your AI-citation status immediately across all platforms - expect Grok and Perplexity to reflect content changes within days, but budget three-plus weeks before ChatGPT and Claude citations catch up, and treat Brave Search's own index as a distinct target worth checking for Claude visibility specifically.

### 17. How deep LLMs actually read the SERP - three pages, three or four queries  `60.8`
*core · content insights · source 60*

This is the clearest statement in the corpus on how deep to aim. Joe's model: when an LLM does a web search it only does shallow research, going to pages two or three - but it will do that on three or four different search terms, so there are perhaps 60 or 70 websites it could look at. That gives you decent surface area. His conclusion for practitioners is direct: if you want to influence AI, you really want your placement ranking in those top three or four pages, otherwise it's redundant. He would not commission a brand new listicle on a DR10 site with no traffic, because it will never get picked up. The exception he allows is that when LLMs update their foundational knowledge they may scrape much further than pages one to three - so a non-ranking placement might help, but he wouldn't count on it. ### 19. Inspect live query fan-outs via Chrome DevTools  `20.6`

4. Submit a test prompt in the chat interface (e.g., 'What time does the Empire State Building open?').
5. In the Network tab's request list, look at the 'Name' column for entries related to a 'conversations' endpoint.
6. Click into one of these conversation entries and inspect its response payload for a field labeled 'search.'
7. Read the 'search' field's contents: the literal sub-queries (the query fan-out) generated for that prompt, along with each query's source and source type.
8. Repeat the same prompt multiple times, or across different times of day, since results are probabilistic and will vary between runs. (inferred, per the source's note that dedicated tools average multiple daily runs)

### 22. Kill vague and inconsistent messaging across all public listings  `69.7`
*core · best practices · source 69*

Smarty calls inconsistent and often vague messaging one of the biggest AI optimization mistakes out there. Her fix is a sweep rather than a page edit: update the About page and all your public business listings with specific, clear and consistent messaging at the same time. The reasoning connects to how models answer. An answer is assembled from what several sources agree on, so three different descriptions of your business split the signal three ways and none of them reaches the threshold to be repeated. Vagueness compounds the problem, because a description that names no category and no customer cannot be matched to any prompt.

> "inconsistent, and often vague messaging"

**How to do it**

5. Use the same category term everywhere; pick one name for what you sell and never alternate synonyms.
6. Log the update date per listing and re-sweep every six months.
7. Ask an AI assistant to describe your company and check whether the answer matches the canonical wording.

**Pitfall:** Fixing the website and skipping the listings you do not own logins for. Stale third-party profiles keep feeding the old description into the consensus long after the site is updated.

**Apply at Pabau:** Pabau should run one sweep across G2, Capterra, LinkedIn, the App Store listing for Pabau GO and every partner directory, aligning the category term and the one-line description with the About page.

### 23. LLMs answer with consensus, not fact-checked truth  `18.3`

> "it's giving you the consensus and the vast majority"

**Apply at Pabau:** For AI visibility (GEO), Pabau should aim to build a critical mass of consistent, aligned content and third-party mentions around its core category claims, since LLM answers appear to be swayed by which side of a topic has produced more aligned content, not which is objectively correct.
